Chapter One

The Terms of Peace

The Red Keep, before sunrise

The third day of the ninth moon

The realm had mistaken silence for peace.

The ravens came overnight. By dawn, the rookery smelled of wet feathers, cold stone, and the sharp ink of a dozen hurried messages. Maester Gerardys sorted them by urgency, setting aside the routine correspondence from coastal lords and placing the sealed messages from the Red Keep on top.

There were two names on the final parchment. Jacaerys Velaryon. Aegon Targaryen. The proposed match was written in the careful hand of a man who believed that a kingdom could be mended with a sentence.

Neither prince had been asked what he wanted. The council had already decided what the realm required.

POV Jacaerys
“A peace built on unwilling vows is still a peace. Until someone decides it is not.” An observation recorded by the court historian

Earlier that morning

Jacaerys stood alone beside the narrow window. Below, the courtyard was waking. Stablehands crossed the stones with buckets, guards changed their watch, and the banners stirred in a wind that carried the smell of rain.

The hall was full of candles.

Every flame bent toward the throne, though there was no wind.

At the far end, someone was calling his name.

He read the proposal once.

Then again, more slowly.

On the third reading, the words did not change. Only his understanding of them did.

To my mother, the Queen,

I have read the council's terms. I understand the danger they seek to prevent, and I will not pretend that the danger is imaginary.

But I ask that you remember the difference between a promise made freely and one extracted in the name of duty. The realm may require my answer. It cannot make that answer painless.

Your devoted son,

Jacaerys
