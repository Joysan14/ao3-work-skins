PresentThe Kappa Nu Alpha house

The first thing Todd Stevens noticed about the fraternity house was how carefully it kept its secrets. From the street, it looked much like every other old building on campus: handsome brickwork, white columns, windows polished to a shine. Inside, however, every room seemed to hold a different version of the truth.

He had been there often enough to know where the floorboards complained, which doors stuck in damp weather, and how quickly a pleasant conversation could turn into a negotiation.

Ben Taylor stood by the staircase, sleeves rolled to his elbows. He looked entirely at home. Todd found that irritating.

Todd watched Ben cross the room. Why does he always have to make this difficult? He looked down at the glass in his hand, pretending the question had never occurred to him.

Ben said his name, and Todd looked up. Not now. Please, not now. There was no point in pretending he had not heard.

Todd knew the house well enough to recognise the performance. The polished banister, the framed photographs, the careful arrangement of trophies. Everything had been put in its proper place. Everyone, it seemed, was expected to do the same.

Ben never appeared to be performing. That was what made him so difficult to dismiss.

He thinks he can make this simple. He thinks being decent is enough.

I should have left before he got here.

He could make a room believe in him. That was the trick, wasn't it? Not being good. Looking as though goodness came naturally.

For a moment, the house was younger. The staircase was crowded with boys in borrowed ties, and somebody was laughing in the kitchen. The memory arrived without warning, bright and incomplete.

Then the present returned, with all its debts.
