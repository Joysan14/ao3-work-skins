The Presidency

At Sumpter College, some places belonged to everyone. Others only pretended to

The Kappa Nu Alpha house was the second kind.

It sat on top of College Hill, above the brick lecture halls and the bike racks. Six white columns held up the porch roof. The front steps were wide enough for a dozen brothers to lounge on at once.

The lawn was cut every Thursday. The brass Greek letters over the door were polished until they caught the afternoon sun and threw it back at anyone walking past.

Students walked past all day. Some stared up at it with hunger. Some muttered under their breath. A few kept their eyes on their phones and pretended it was just a building.

Inside, the air smelled of lemon polish and old beer. Hardwood floors sagged a little in the common room, worn down by decades of parties.

Ben Taylor Kappa Nu Alpha House · Late afternoon

Ben had learned that a room could make a person feel unwelcome without anyone saying a word. It was in the furniture, the framed photographs, the way conversations paused when he entered.

He kept his hands in his pockets and tried not to look as though he was counting the exits.
