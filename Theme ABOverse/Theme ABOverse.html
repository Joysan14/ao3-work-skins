Chapter One: The Quiet Hour

The rain had stopped before dawn, leaving the streets polished black beneath the old lamps. Somewhere beyond the square, a clock struck six.

He had not slept. The letter lay open on the desk, its paper creased where his fingers had worried the same sentence all night.

Austin

There were things a person could explain, and things that became less true each time they were spoken aloud.

I should have burned the letter. I should have burned it the moment it arrived.

He turned the envelope over once more, searching for a detail he had somehow missed. There was nothing. No signature. No address. Only the seal.

What if it was not a warning?

What if someone was asking him to come back?

He closed his eyes. For one brief moment, he could almost hear his sister's voice again.

Don't go back there, Austin. Whatever you remember, leave it buried.

But memory had never been something he could leave where it was.

The envelope bore no return address. Only a small mark pressed into the wax: three lines crossing beneath a crown.

Years earlier, he had seen the same mark on the door of the house at the end of the lane.

He remembered the smell of wet wool, the fire burning low, and a voice telling him not to look back.

In the dream, the hallway stretched farther each time he tried to reach the door.
